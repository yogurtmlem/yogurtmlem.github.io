# Thought bubble 1. Learning Japanese
Hi, I decided that learning Japanese would be the topic of my first blog post. I'll briefly talk about why I started learning Japanese and we'll see where it goes from there I don't have anything planned beforehand.
## Why
Hazy Moon by Hatsune Miku + Kodai Fujimoto. The first one is probably what gave me the idea, the later one is the push that i needed to finally start learning.
## How
The first hurdle is learning the alphabets, Hiragana and Katakana. This took me around 2 weeks to really get them down. Tofugu is a great source, their image-based mnemonics are helpful for those who are not familiar with non-Latin writing systems. I recommend drilling the Tofugu’s Learn Kana Quiz until you get only 1 or 2 wrong.  

Next, you can either use an app, pdf scans of a textbook or the physical version to start learning. Call me old fashioned but I firmly believe it's best to start out with physical textbooks, that extra step of buying a book helped me stay commited after the honeymoon phase had passed. That said, any method works fine, it all comes down to whether or not you can turn it into a habit. I picked Minna no Nihongo 1 and 2 because that was the first thing that caught my eye when I googled "Japanese books for beginners Reddit", plus it was on sale on Shopee so buying it was a no brainer. Free lessons posted on Youtube cover pretty much everything you need to **pass** the N3.
> *Do not do what I did*. Speaking from personal experience, Minna no Nihongo is great if your goal is to pass the JLPT + have a strong grasp on Japanese grammar structures + have a solid vocabulary. If your goal is, for example, to be able to hold a conversation in Japanese within xx months, other books like Marugoto would be better suited for you (or so I've heard).

I usually need 2 days to get through one lesson, one for learning grammar and vocabulary the other for doing exercise and drilling flashcards. I went through my options: Quizlet, Anki, Kahoot,... Quizlet has a lot of useful features locked behind a paywall, same case applied to Kahoot which left me with Anki. It's free, have built-in space repitition with a wide range of add-ons. With Anki you can download pre-made vocabulary decks from Anki community or make them yourself, I opted for the second option. 
> I originally planned to learn Japanese using English since I'd taken notice of my declining ability to think and write in English. 10 lessons in and I could no longer ignore the fact that it is objectively better to learn Japanese using Vietnamese (both are Sinosphere languages with a lot of Chinese loan words) so I made the switch. This was extremely on the nose I was just stubborn and pretentious.
