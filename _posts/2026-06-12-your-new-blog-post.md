# Thought bubble 1. 
## Learning Japanese
Hi, I decided that learning Japanese would be the topic of my first blog post. I'll briefly talk about why I started learning Japanese and we'll see where it goes from there I don't have anything planned beforehand.
### Why
Hazy Moon by Hatsune Miku + Kodai Fujimoto. The first one is probably what gave me the idea, the later one is the push that i needed to finally start learning.
### How
The first hurdle is learning the alphabets, Hiragana and Katakana. This took me around 2 weeks to really get them down. Tofugu is a great source, their image-based mnemonics are helpful
for those who are not familiar with non-Latin writing systems. I recommend drilling the Tofugu’s Learn Kana Quiz until you get only 1 or 2 wrong. 

Next, you need to decide on a roadmap. Different people 
Call me old fashioned but I firmly believe that the best way to learn the basics is through physical textbooks.    
Free lessons posted on Youtube. They cover pretty much everything you need to pass the N3. I picked Minna no Nihongo 1 and 2 because 
that was the first thing that caught my eye when I googled "Japanese books for beginners Reddit", plus it was on sale on Shopee so buying it was a no brainer. 
> *Do not do what I did*. Speaking from personal experience, Minna no Nihongo is great if your goal is to pass the JLPT + have a strong grasp on Japanese grammar
structures + have a solid vocabulary. If your goal is, for example, to be able to hold a conversation in Japanese within xx months, other books like Marugoto
would be better suited for you (or so I've heard).
